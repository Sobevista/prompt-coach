Prompt Coach

Learn to talk to AI so it actually helps you. No experience needed.

Start here

AI is not magic and it can't read your mind. It's more like a brilliant new coworker who knows a lot but knows nothing about you or your situation until you tell them. Prompting is just the skill of telling them well.

Here's the encouraging part: this is a practical skill you can practise, and it's not tricks. This course organizes it into three habits that build on each other. You'll have tried all three by the end of this page.

This course was shaped by one person's progression from simple questions to more structured prompting. Treat the three habits as a practical teaching model to test on your work, not proof that everyone follows the same path.

The 3 habits (the whole game)

Everything else is a version of one of these. Learn them in order.

1

Give the givens

The AI can only work with what you hand it. Front-load your real situation — the facts, numbers, and constraints — before you ask.

WeakWrite me a job ad.
StrongWrite a job ad for a landscaping crew member. Pay $19/hr, full-time Mon–Sat. Must lift 50 lbs and have reliable transport. Tone: straight-talking.
2

Ask for a shape

Tell it what form the answer should take, or you'll get a shapeless blob. Steps? A table? Three options? One yes/no?

WeakHelp me plan an offsite.
StrongPlan a 1-day offsite for 12 people. Give me back: 3 theme options, a sample hour-by-hour schedule, and a rough budget.
3

Don't trust the first answer

The first reply is a draft, not gospel. Ask for checkable assumptions, criteria, sources, calculations, uncertainty or a verification step; argue the other side; or ask for options.

WeakThanks, I'll use that. (to a generic answer)
StrongThat's too generic. Ask me 3 questions, then give me 3 stronger versions to choose from.

The 7 moves

These are the three habits, broken into specific things you can actually type. You don't need all seven at once — reach for whichever fits.

Practice

Part 1 — Spot it. Read each situation and pick what the prompt needs most. You'll get instant feedback. There's often a defensible second choice — we're after the strongest one.

Part 2 — Write it

Spotting the fix is half the skill; typing it is the other half. Rewrite each weak prompt yourself, then reveal a strong version and compare. There's no single right answer — check yours against the checklist.
